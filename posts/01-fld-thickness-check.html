Previewing thickness reduction with a forming limit diagram

2026.08.12 · Ventron

During press forming, sheet metal stretches and thins by different amounts in different places. The forming limit diagram (FLD) is a way to judge that change in one chart.

What is a forming limit diagram

An FLD plots the two in-plane strains of the sheet, major and minor strain, and compares them with the limit curve the material can withstand. Plot each point from the simulation on it, and you can see at a glance which areas are close to fracture.

Three zones used in practice

  • Safe zone: well clear of the limit curve. No action is needed.
  • Caution zone: close to the limit curve. Adjust process conditions (blank shape, pressure, lubrication) to gain margin.
  • Risk zone: beyond the limit curve. The die geometry or the sheet thickness itself may need to change.

Why check at the drawing stage

If fracture is found after the die is built, correcting the geometry and remaking the die costs time and money again. If the risk zones are found earlier with an FLD in simulation, the fixes can be settled before the die is made.

When Ventron writes this up in a report, it gives the location and cause of each risk zone, along with the options for adjustment (geometry and process conditions).
